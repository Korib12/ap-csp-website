What you're checking

Read the spectrogram bottom-to-top as low-to-high frequency; time scrolls right to left; brightness is energy. A working phone shows a steady bright horizontal line exactly at the emit frequency (the orange marker). That line is your sonar carrier — everything in Echo rides on it.

1
See the carrier. Start the test and hold the phone still. Pass: a crisp, stable bright line sits right at the orange marker. If the line is missing, smeared, or flickers wildly even when still, this phone's speaker/mic can't hold the band — note it and try the other phone or a lower frequency.
2
See the Doppler. Keep the phone still and slowly wave your hand 15–30 cm above it. Pass: the line briefly fattens or grows fuzzy sidebands as your hand moves — that blur is the reflected echo shifting frequency. This is the effect breathing will make, only far bigger. If a moving hand does nothing at all, the mic isn't picking up the reflection.
3
Find the quiet range. Prop the phone ~30 cm from your chest, stay still, and breathe deeply. Pass: a faint slow shimmer on the line synced to your breath. This one is hard and hardware-dependent — treat it as bonus, not the gate. Tests 1 and 2 are the real go/no-go.
Green light if tests 1 and 2 pass on both phones: your hardware cooperates and Echo is worth committing to. If test 1 fails on both even after lowering the frequency, that's your signal to fall back to Pulse (rPPG) — same pipeline, friendlier hardware.
