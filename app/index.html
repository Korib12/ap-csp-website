echo

A breathing monitor that works with sound, for the people you worry about at night.

Scroll to see how it works

Echo sends out a steady sound around 17 kHz and listens for it to bounce back. No camera, nothing to wear.

As the chest rises and falls, the echo shifts by a fraction of a millimeter. Echo turns that shift into breaths.

If they roll away or a blanket blocks the sound, Echo stops recording instead of guessing. Pauses of 10 seconds or more are logged.

When to talk to a doctor

Signs of sleep apnea listed by the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute include:

  • Breathing that starts and stops during sleep
  • Frequent loud snoring
  • Gasping for air
  • Daytime sleepiness or tiredness
  • Headaches or dry mouth
  • Waking up often at night to urinate

Echo can't diagnose anything, but a Morning Report showing repeated pauses is a good way to start the conversation. Share it from the Reports tab.

Medicare and sleep apnea

  • Testing: a doctor orders the sleep test. Medicare accepts a sleep-lab study or certain home sleep tests.
  • CPAP machine: Medicare may cover a 12-week trial. It keeps covering it if a doctor sees the patient and documents that it's helping.
  • Rental: Medicare rents the machine for 13 months of continuous use, then you own it.
  • Cost: after the Part B deductible, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount if the supplier accepts assignment. Medicare Advantage plans can differ.

HICAP gives free, one-on-one Medicare counseling in California.

Alerts (experimental)

Off by default. Echo is built to record data for a morning report. Alerts are experimental: rolling over or a thick blanket can cause false alarms, and real events can be missed.
Sleep doctors count a pause of 10 seconds or more as a breathing event. A longer setting means fewer false alarms.
