echo

A breathing monitor that works with sound, for the people you worry about at night.

Scroll to see how it works

Your phone plays a tone too high to hear.

Echo sends out a steady sound around 17 kHz and listens for it to bounce back. No camera, nothing to wear.

Every breath moves the echo.

As the chest rises and falls, the echo shifts by a fraction of a millimeter. Echo turns that shift into breaths.

It records only what it can see clearly.

If they roll away or a blanket blocks the sound, Echo stops recording instead of guessing. Pauses of 10 seconds or more are logged.

Tonight

Echo records breathing overnight with sound you can't hear, for a morning report. No camera, nothing to wear, and nothing to sign up for.

  1. Plug in the phone. Echo keeps the screen on all night.
  2. Works best for side sleepers. Put the phone on the nightstand at mattress height, about 30–50 cm away, with the bottom edge facing the person's chest. Sonar reflects off the body like a mirror, so the phone has to face the chest straight on.
  3. Turn the phone's volume up and take out any earbuds.
  4. Tap Start. Echo tunes itself, then records only while it can clearly see breathing. If the person rolls away or a thick blanket blocks it, Echo pauses recording instead of guessing.

Alerts (experimental)

Off by default. Echo is built to record data for a morning report. Alerts are experimental: rolling over or a thick blanket can cause false alarms, and real events can be missed.
Sleep doctors count a pause of 10 seconds or more as a breathing event. A longer setting means fewer false alarms.
Normal is the level used in the accuracy tests. High is louder, which may help at longer distances, but can distort on some phones. Applies the next time you start.
